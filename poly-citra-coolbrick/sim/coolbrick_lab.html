How the simulation works

Each wall is split into 47 thin slices. Every 10 seconds of simulated time the program works out how much heat moves between neighbouring slices (q = k·ΔT/Δx). On the outside, the wall takes in sunlight (α·I), swaps heat with the air (h = 15 W/m²K), and radiates heat to a sky about 18 °C colder than the air. On the inside it meets 25 °C room air (h = 8 W/m²K). The phase-change layer stores extra heat as it melts. The model runs three days and shows the third, so the starting guess doesn't matter.

Model room and fans-off test

  • 3 × 3 × 3 m room: roof, east and west walls 9 m² each, north and south walls 18 m² together.
  • Roof and walls use the chosen material.
  • Fans-off test: from 14:00 the cooling stops. People and lights keep heating the room air and furniture (about 230 kJ/K), and that heat soaks into the walls.

Read this before quoting numbers

These are model estimates built from assumed material values, not measurements. The CoolBrick values are design targets. Swap in your own lab results (conductivity, density, the salt's melting point and stored heat) and repeat the two-model-room experiment to confirm them.
